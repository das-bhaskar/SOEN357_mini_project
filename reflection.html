Project Reflection

Lessons learned in accessibility, iteration, and user-centric design.

Design Challenges

The primary challenge of the Health Companion project was balancing two polar opposite user needs within a single design system:

  • The Caregiver (Sunita): Needed high-density information, rapid biometric entry (FaceID), and quick-access emergency records.
  • The Senior (Rajesh): Needed extreme simplicity, high-contrast visuals, and "error-proof" tactile interactions.

Initially, my designs felt too "clinical" and heavy. The UX Research phase was critical here; it helped me move beyond my own assumptions and realize that medical apps don't have to look intimidating. By following an iterative design process, I was able to pivot to a "Warm Sunset" palette, transforming the app into a supportive lifestyle tool rather than just a digital medical form.

Key Takeaways

Three core principles that shaped the final prototype.

01. Accessibility is not Optional

Testing with the Rajesh persona taught me that button size is a functional requirement, not a visual preference. Increasing touch targets and adding the 3-second hold changed the app from frustrating to reliable.

02. Respect the Hardware

Designing in Figma is different from using a real phone. Realizing that the iPhone Notch blocked my navigation was a epiphany that forced me to understand Safe Area margins in UI development.

03. The Power of Feedback

User testing revealed "dead-ends" in my navigation. Adding simple back-buttons seems obvious now, but it was the most requested fix from my participants. It taught me that as a designer, I am often too close to the project to see simple flaws.
